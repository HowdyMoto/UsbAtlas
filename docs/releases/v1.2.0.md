USB Atlas 1.2.0 draws every port as the socket it is, keeps the two halves of a USB 3 socket together, and adds renaming on the canvas, port names, linked hub stages, a focused view for large topologies, and polling rates and power-saving checks for sim racing and other game controllers.

## What's new

- Ports are drawn as their sockets: USB-A as a rectangle with its tongue along the top, USB-C as a pill with its tongue in the middle, and built-in connections with nothing to plug into as plain slots.
- Each socket's tongue shows its speed in USB's color code: black for USB 2, blue for USB 3 at 5 Gb/s, and red for 10 Gb/s or faster. Windows doesn't report an empty port's top rate, so a socket turns red only once a device links at SuperSpeedPlus or its hub supports it. A legend beneath the graph shows each kind of socket.
- Windows reports each USB 3 socket as two logical ports, one USB 2 and one USB 3. Both halves are now drawn as the physical socket they share. On the same hub they sit side by side as one socket split at a seam; each half still selects and connects on its own, and a filled half shows which one a device is using.
- USB 3 hubs that Windows shows as a separate USB 2 hub and USB 3 hub, including built-in ones, are recognized as two sides of one hub and labeled on their cards. The USB 2 side no longer shows a Reduced speed warning at its native 480 Mb/s.
- Rename ports and devices right on the canvas: double-click a socket or a card's name, press F2, or right-click. Properties uses the same editor. A named port shows as a small tag on the card plugged into it, or beside an empty socket. Port names stay with the port when devices change, appear in the tree and are searchable.
- A self-powered hub, or a device that draws nothing from the bus, reads **External power** (or **External + 100 mA** when it also draws a little) instead of a misleading 0 mA.
- Keyboards, mice, HID controls and game controllers show their polling rate on their cards, such as 1000 Hz, and every device shows it in Properties. It is how often the host asks for input, from the device's endpoint descriptor, not a measured report rate.
- Wheels, pedals, shifters, handbrakes, button boxes, joysticks and game pads are recognized as game controllers by the joystick and game pad HID collections Windows reports for them, and by sim racing and flight sim brands and the parts of a rig. Properties lists each device's HID collections.
- Properties shows each device's “Allow the computer to turn off this device to save power” setting and the power plan's USB selective suspend setting. A game controller that Windows may suspend is flagged **Selective suspend on**, with how to turn it off. USB Atlas only reads these settings.
- **Snap to upstream hub** places the chips of a multi-stage hub side by side inside an outline while keeping the real connections. The first hub in a chain lists its downstream stages, so they can be snapped or unlinked from there.
- Large topologies open on a focused branch and its upstream path; **Show all branches** and **Focus branch** switch between views.
- The inspector is now **Properties**. Click a warning to see what it means, including reconnect times for unstable connections.
- Larger sockets, cards and text, and clearer view controls: **100% view**, **Fit all**, **Center selection** and **Layout**.
- Search also matches port names and socket types, such as “USB-C” or “10 Gb/s”, polling rates such as “1000 Hz”, and HID collections such as “Joystick”.
- Sample hardware moved from the toolbar to the `--demo` command-line option, and now includes a direct-drive wheel base.

## Download and run

Download **UsbAtlas-1.2.0-win-x64.zip**, extract the entire archive, and open **UsbAtlas.exe**. For Windows 10/11 x64. Includes .NET 10 Desktop Runtime 10.0.11; no separate runtime installation is needed.

The packaged executable passed self-tests and offscreen WPF UI checks in light and dark themes at default, compact and wide window sizes and in the horizontal layout, including socket shapes and tongue colors, split sockets in both layouts, hub pairing, linked hub stages, renaming on the canvas and crossing-free routing. Sockets were also checked against a real scan with USB-C and USB-A sockets, built-in ports and a built-in USB 3 hub. A SHA-256 checksum is included.
