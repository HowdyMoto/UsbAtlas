USB Atlas 1.2.0 draws every port as the socket it is, keeps the two halves of a USB 3 socket together, and adds renaming on the canvas, port names, linked hub stages, a focused view for large topologies, and polling rates and power-saving checks for sim racing and other game controllers.

## What's new

- Ports are drawn as their sockets: USB-A as a rectangle with its tongue along the top, USB-C as a pill with its tongue in the middle, and built-in connections with nothing to plug into as plain slots.
- Each socket's tongue shows its speed in USB's color code: black for USB 2, blue for USB 3 at 5 Gb/s, and red for 10 Gb/s or faster. Windows doesn't report an empty port's top rate, so a socket turns red only once a device links at SuperSpeedPlus or its hub supports it. A legend beneath the graph shows each kind of socket.
- Windows reports each USB 3 socket as two logical ports, one USB 2 and one USB 3. Both halves are now drawn as the physical socket they share. On the same hub they sit side by side as one socket split at a seam; each half still selects and connects on its own, and a filled half shows which one a device is using.
- USB 3 hubs that Windows shows as a separate USB 2 hub and USB 3 hub, including built-in ones, are recognized as two sides of one hub and labeled on their cards. The USB 2 side is no longer flagged as slow at its native 480 Mb/s, and Detection details no longer tell you to check its cable.
- Properties now starts with what an issue means in plain words: what is happening, whether it affects anything plugged in now, and what to do, most likely cause first. Speed comes first: **Running at USB 2** and **Running at 5 Gb/s** replace Reduced speed, and point to the likely cause, whether that's the port, a slower hub upstream, the cable, or a monitor using its USB-C lanes for the display.
- Every warning now explains itself the same way, and the alarm level matches the stakes. A nearly full link, a nearly full shared TT, and a hub running on bus power whose devices still fit are notes; peaks that can't all fit, power at risk and failures stay amber or red.
- KVM switches, monitor input changes and undocking no longer flag every device: when a hub drops, everything behind it goes with it, so only the hub is flagged, with that explanation first. A device behind it is still flagged for drops of its own.
- Properties uses plainer words: hubs read **Plug-in hub** or **Built-in hub**, rows are named Link speed, USB version, Path, Power available, Devices behind and Slower devices, with each label's tooltip naming the technical term, and explanations describe devices as people do, such as “your keyboard, mouse and 1 other device”.
- Notes: a calm gray badge with an outlined ring marks what's worth knowing but affects nothing now, such as a hub running at USB 2 with only USB 2 devices behind it, or a built-in connection that can't be changed. Notes are counted apart from issues.
- A USB 3 hub whose USB 3 side didn't connect, such as a monitor's hub when the monitor gives the USB-C cable's fast lanes to the display, shows **Running at USB 2**: Windows reports the USB 3 half of its socket empty, or showing an error when the USB 3 side tried and failed, so the hub and everything behind it run at USB 2.
- Hubs with one transaction translator (TT) for all their ports are recognized. Full- and low-speed devices behind such a hub, such as audio interfaces, MIDI gear and many game controllers, share one 12 Mb/s bus, so devices that each fit their own link can still be refused together. Beside the hub's link rate, **Shared TT nearly full** is a note while everything still fits, and **Shared TT could exceed** warns when their peaks can't all fit. Properties shows each hub's **Slower devices** row (“Share one link · single TT” or “Link per port · multi-TT”) and how full a single TT is.
- Rename ports and devices right on the canvas: double-click a socket or a card's name, press F2, or right-click. Properties uses the same editor. A named port shows as a small tag on the card plugged into it, or beside an empty socket. Port names stay with the port when devices change, appear in the tree and are searchable.
- A self-powered hub, or a device that draws nothing from the bus, reads **External power** (or **External + 100 mA** when it also draws a little) instead of a misleading 0 mA.
- Keyboards, mice, HID controls and game controllers show their polling rate on their cards, such as 1000 Hz, and every device shows it in Properties. It is how often the host asks for input, from the device's endpoint descriptor, not a measured report rate.
- Wheels, pedals, shifters, handbrakes, button boxes, joysticks and game pads are recognized as game controllers by the joystick and game pad HID collections Windows reports for them, and by sim racing and flight sim brands and the parts of a rig. Properties lists each device's HID collections.
- Properties shows each device's “Allow the computer to turn off this device to save power” setting and the power plan's USB selective suspend setting. A game controller that Windows may suspend is flagged **Selective suspend on**, with how to turn it off. USB Atlas only reads these settings.
- **Snap to upstream hub** places the chips of a multi-stage hub side by side inside an outline while keeping the real connections. The first hub in a chain lists its downstream stages, so they can be snapped or unlinked from there.
- Large topologies open on a focused branch and its upstream path; **Show all branches** and **Focus branch** switch between views.
- The inspector is now **Properties**. Click a warning to see what it means, including reconnect times for unstable connections.
- Larger sockets, cards and text, and clearer view controls: **100% view**, **Fit all**, **Center selection** and **Layout**.
- Search also matches port names and socket types, such as “USB-C” or “10 Gb/s”, polling rates such as “1000 Hz”, and HID collections such as “Joystick”.
- Sample hardware moved from the toolbar to the `--demo` command-line option, and now includes a direct-drive wheel base.
- Port map notes: a computer's firmware tells Windows which built-in ports can be plugged into, which are USB-C, and which USB 2 and USB 3 ports share a socket. Where that description contradicts itself, such as a USB 3 port with no USB 2 half, or two ports paired one way only, the port shows a note that names the ports involved and says nothing plugged in is affected.
- For people who build or test boards: `usbatlas-cli check` passes or fails on those contradictions, `usbatlas-cli map --out board.json` saves a known-good unit's port map with what is wired in, and `usbatlas-cli check board.json` compares another unit or firmware build against it, one line per mismatch. The map can be edited, and `--devices` adds what is plugged in for a test fixture.
- Host controllers that share a name are told apart by their PCI identity: Properties shows each one's chip maker, vendor:device IDs and bus:device.function address, as do `tree` and `show`.
- A SuperSpeedPlus link shows its rate, 10 Gb/s or 20 Gb/s, and its lanes when Windows reports them, in place of “10 Gb/s or higher”, and its bandwidth meter uses that rate.
- `usbatlas-cli watch` reports when the computer sleeps and wakes, and what didn't come back or came back on a slower link. Devices returning as the computer wakes are no longer counted toward an unstable connection there. `--out FILE` keeps every event as a line of JSON.

## Download and run

Download **UsbAtlas-1.2.0-win-x64.zip**, extract the entire archive, and open **UsbAtlas.exe**. For Windows 10/11 x64. Includes .NET 10 Desktop Runtime 10.0.11; no separate runtime installation is needed.

The packaged executable passed self-tests and offscreen WPF UI checks in light and dark themes at default, compact and wide window sizes and in the horizontal layout, including socket shapes and tongue colors, split sockets in both layouts, hub pairing, linked hub stages, renaming on the canvas and crossing-free routing. Sockets were also checked against a real scan with USB-C and USB-A sockets, built-in ports and a built-in USB 3 hub. A SHA-256 checksum is included.
