USB Atlas 1.3.0 opens on your whole setup, draws each connection's speed into its wire, and finds three more kinds of trouble: USB 2 devices stuck at 12 Mb/s, controllers held back by their PCIe link, and controllers running out of endpoints. It also gets along with KVM switches.

## What's new

- The app opens on the whole topology. Cards have Full, Compact and Far levels; opening and **Fit all** pick the most detailed level that fits, and the mouse wheel switches levels around the card under the pointer.
- Wires show their link: wider for faster links, dashed when slower than the device supports, and amber when that holds something back.
- Cards are neutral, and a device's category shows in its icon's color.
- A hub's bandwidth meter is split into a part for each device on its ports. Hovering a part rings that card, and hovering a card lights its part. The label gives shares of what the link can reserve; the rates are in its tooltip.
- A USB 3 hub's two sides are drawn as one card when nothing is wired between their ports. A missing USB 3 side shows as a dashed amber stub on its empty socket half.
- Horizontal layout is the default, and the toolbar's **Horizontal | Vertical** control switches and remembers it. In the horizontal layout, far cards pack a hub's four or more end devices into two columns; in the vertical layout, a hub's devices always stay in one row.
- When a scan changes what's plugged in, cards slide to their new places, new ones fade in and removed ones fade out where they stood. Windows' animation setting turns this off.
- In the horizontal layout, a card widens to fit its name; longer names wrap onto a second line on full cards.
- The legend groups its entries by Speed, Socket and Link. Narrowing the window folds the device tree away, and widening brings it back.
- Zoom has fewer controls: the zoom readout shows 100%, and Shift+0, Shift+1 and Shift+2 show 100%, fit all and center the selection.
- Properties sizes explanations by severity: a note is one line, a warning keeps what to do a click away, and an error is shown whole.
- **Running at 12 Mb/s**: a USB 2 device linked at 12 Mb/s is asked for its high-speed details, which a full-speed-only device must refuse. One that answers supports 480 Mb/s, and Properties names the cause: a USB 1.1 hub on the way, often an old hub or a KVM switch, or a cable or extension that couldn't hold high speed.
- **Limited by PCIe link**: Properties shows each controller's **PCIe link**, such as “PCIe 2.0 ×1 · about 4 Gb/s”, and what it could do when its slot holds it back. A device linked faster than that link warns; ports that could only outrun it together get a note. Add-in cards are where this bites.
- **Many endpoints in use**: Properties shows each controller's **Endpoints** in use. Controllers hold only so many, some as few as 96, and Windows then refuses the next device with “Not enough USB controller resources”. From 64, the controller gets a note saying where to move a device.
- KVM switches: when a hub drops and returns with everything behind it, as switching a KVM, changing monitor inputs or undocking does, only the hub is flagged, as a note. A device dropping on its own is still a warning. Reconnect times use your system's time format everywhere.
- A monitor hub whose USB 3 side is missing now covers monitors with no USB-C setting: USB over that cable stays at USB 2, so fast devices belong on the computer.
- A port Windows is still setting up reads **Still connecting**, a note, instead of an unknown 0000:0000 device at 1.5 Mb/s.

### Command line

- `usbatlas-cli watch` waits for a change to settle, rescanning until two scans agree, so problem codes that clear in a second, drivers still loading and half-read figures are no longer reported.
- Unplugging a device no longer counts as resolving its issues, in `watch` and `diff`. A watch remembers what it reported, so an issue isn't announced again when its device comes back, and is resolved when the device returns without it.
- `watch` reads “watching until stopped” when it has no end, and counts are singular where they should be.
- `show` on a controller reports its PCIe link and endpoints in use; a link reports whether a device is high-speed capable.
- `run-cli.ps1` builds and runs the command line from source, and works while the app is open.

## Download and run

Download **UsbAtlas-1.3.0-win-x64.zip**, extract the entire archive, and open **UsbAtlas.exe**. The command line, **usbatlas-cli.exe**, is in the same folder. For Windows 10/11 x64. Includes the .NET 10 Desktop Runtime; no separate runtime installation is needed.
